# 📖 March 2026 Reading Log

*The curiosities that caught my attention this month*

## Health & Fitness Deep Dives

A four-part series exploring the science and practice of human movement, strength, and endurance. Detailed, humorous, and full of analogies for those of us who find dry textbooks about as inspiring as a stale rice cake.

### [The Art of Not Being a Stiff Board: Stretching, Flexibility & Mobility](../by-topic/health-fitness-flexibility.md)
*Original Article • March 2026*

Started here because flexibility is the most neglected component of fitness and also the one that will matter most at 70. The distinction between flexibility (passive) and mobility (active, controlled) was a genuine lightbulb moment — like realising your long leash is useless without a trained dog on the end of it. The section on the nervous system's role in stretch limits was eye-opening: most of what we feel as "tightness" is the brain being cautious, not the tissue being short.

**The takeaway that stuck:** PNF stretching — contracting then relaxing to trick the Golgi tendon organ — is genuinely clever and genuinely more effective than just holding a stretch and hoping.

### [The Magnificent Machine: Human Anatomy for Movement & Injury Prevention](../by-topic/health-fitness-anatomy.md)
*Original Article • March 2026*

The joint-by-joint model (alternating stability and mobility joints up the kinetic chain) completely reframes injury. Lower back pain? Look at the hips. Knee pain? Look at the hip AND the ankle. It's like debugging code by tracing the error upstream rather than just patching the line that threw the exception.

**The revelation:** The glute medius is responsible for preventing the pelvis from dropping when running on one leg. Weak glute med is quietly behind a staggering percentage of lower limb injuries — IT band syndrome, runner's knee, lower back pain. One muscle, enormous consequences.

### [Gravity Is Your Barbell: Progressions & Leverage in Bodyweight Training](../by-topic/health-fitness-bodyweight.md)
*Original Article • March 2026*

The physics section (moment arms, leverage, effective load) made the whole planche-as-extreme-push-up thing click in a way that no number of YouTube tutorials had managed. You're not adding weight — you're extending the lever. The same mass becomes harder to move as the moment arm increases. Simple, elegant, humbling.

**Immediate plan:** Start working Bulgarian split squats seriously and learn the hollow body hold. They appear to be foundational for everything else interesting.

### [The Long Game: Endurance Fitness for Running, Surfing & Not Giving Up](../by-topic/health-fitness-endurance.md)
*Original Article • March 2026*

The CO2 tolerance section for surfing and breath-holding was a revelation. The urge to breathe is driven by CO2 rise, not oxygen depletion — which means most breath-hold discomfort is trainable, not a hard limit. The polarised training model (80% genuinely easy, 20% genuinely hard, almost nothing in between) directly contradicts how most amateur runners actually train, and is backed by solid research on what elites actually do.

**The quote that will haunt every future hard training session:** The reserves were there all along; the brain simply wasn't releasing them until the proximity of the finish made it safe to do so. (On why athletes sprint at the end of a race they claimed they had nothing left for.)
