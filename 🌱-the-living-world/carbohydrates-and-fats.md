# Carbohydrates and Fats: Fuel and Structure 🧱

Glucose can become a readily used fuel, a storage polymer or the structural material of a tree. Lipids can store energy, form a cell membrane or act as hormones. These molecules have more jobs than the nutrition panel has room to explain.

Their behaviour depends on molecular structure: which atoms are connected, how they are arranged and how they interact with water.

## 🍬 Why starch is digestible and cellulose is not

Carbohydrates include simple sugars and chains assembled from them. Glucose, fructose and galactose are **monosaccharides**. Sucrose and lactose join two sugar units. **Polysaccharides** contain many units.

Both starch and cellulose are built from glucose. The difference lies in the bonds connecting it:

- Starch has mainly alpha-1,4 linkages, with alpha-1,6 branch points in amylopectin.
- Cellulose has beta-1,4 linkages, producing extended chains that assemble into strong microfibrils.

Human digestive enzymes can break the relevant bonds in starch, but we lack the cellulases needed to digest cellulose directly. Other organisms make these enzymes or depend on microbial partners that do. A cow's ability to use grass owes a great deal to organisms smaller than the cow has probably considered.

Cellulose is one major component of plant walls, alongside other polysaccharides and, in woody tissues, lignin. **Chitin**, made from a nitrogen-containing sugar derivative, supports fungal walls and arthropod exoskeletons. Carbohydrate chemistry contributes to both leaves and lobsters.

## 🌰 Storing glucose without filling a cell with syrup

Plants store carbohydrate as **starch**, a mixture of relatively linear amylose and branched amylopectin. Animals store it as **glycogen**, which is more densely branched.

Branching supplies many ends where enzymes can add or remove glucose units. Polymerisation also greatly reduces the osmotic burden compared with storing the same number of glucose molecules separately. Otherwise, storing fuel would create a substantial water-balance problem.

Liver glycogen can support blood glucose between meals. Muscle glycogen primarily supplies the muscle containing it; muscle lacks the enzyme needed to release substantial free glucose directly into the blood.

Starch accessibility changes with processing. Heating starch with water can disrupt its granular structure, making it easier for enzymes to reach. Cooling can allow some chains to reassociate into less digestible forms. The amount depends on the food and conditions, not simply whether a potato spent the night in the fridge.

That connection between structure and digestion is developed in [food energy](calories-and-food-energy.md).

## 🏷️ Sugars on cell surfaces

Short carbohydrate chains called **glycans** attach to proteins and lipids. They influence folding, stability, interactions and recognition. Proteins called **lectins** recognise particular glycan structures.

ABO blood groups provide a concrete example: the A and B forms differ in terminal sugars added to a precursor structure by specific enzymes. Common O alleles do not produce an active enzyme for either addition. The important distinction is molecular detail on a surface, rather than a different overall kind of blood cell.

Glycans are assembled through enzyme pathways, rather than copied directly from a template like DNA. Their branching and variable linkages make them difficult to characterise. [Glycobiology](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK579918/) studies this additional layer of biological structure and recognition.

## 🛢️ Several very different kinds of lipid

“Lipid” covers chemically diverse molecules that tend to be hydrophobic or amphipathic, meaning they contain both water-compatible and water-avoiding regions.

| Class | Main structural feature | Some roles |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fatty acids | Hydrocarbon chain with a carboxyl group | Fuel and components of other lipids |
| Triglycerides | Three fatty acids attached to glycerol | Concentrated energy storage |
| Phospholipids | Polar head and hydrophobic regions | Membrane formation |
| Sterols | Fused ring structure | Membrane properties and chemical precursors |
| Waxes | Long-chain esters | Protective surface barriers |

Fat stores substantial energy per gram because its carbon is relatively reduced: oxidation transfers more electrons than oxidation of the same mass of typical carbohydrate. Triglycerides also associate with little water, whereas glycogen stores carry considerable water with them. This makes fat effective for compact, longer-term energy storage.

## 🫧 Membranes assemble, then cells manage them

Many phospholipids form bilayers in water, with polar heads exposed and hydrophobic tails inside. The bilayer restricts movement of ions and many polar molecules. Channels, carriers and pumps control transport across it.

Fatty-acid composition influences packing. Cis double bonds introduce bends that tend to prevent close packing; shorter chains and more unsaturation generally increase fluidity under comparable conditions. Cholesterol modifies packing, permeability and fluidity in animal membranes.

A membrane is not a uniform bag. Its two sides can have different compositions, proteins interact with particular lipid environments, and cells constantly remodel it. These differences let one membrane insulate a nerve fibre and another sustain the proton gradient that drives ATP synthesis.

The mechanics belong with [cells and organelles](cells-and-organelles.md); the gradient appears in [cellular energy](digestion-and-cellular-energy.md).

## 🧪 Structure does not prescribe a diet

Cholesterol is a precursor for steroid hormones and bile acids. Humans can synthesise it. We can also make many fatty acids, but cannot make the essential fatty acids linoleic acid and alpha-linolenic acid from scratch.

These are distinct facts. A molecule being essential to cellular function does not mean more dietary intake is always beneficial, or that the body must obtain that molecule directly from food. Nutritional questions depend on synthesis, absorption, transport and regulation as well as chemical roles.

The useful biological lesson is broader: energy-bearing molecules can also be indispensable materials. [Plants](plant-biology.md), [immunity](immunity.md) and [protein modification](proteins-and-folding.md) all make use of that versatility.
