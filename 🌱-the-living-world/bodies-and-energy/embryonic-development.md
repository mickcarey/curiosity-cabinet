# How Does One Cell Become a Whole Body? 🥚

You began as one cell. Its descendants eventually made fingers, eyes, bones and a heart. They generally put them in the right places too, without a tiny builder standing in the middle shouting instructions.

An **embryo** is a living thing at an early stage of development, while its basic body structures are forming. The puzzle is how its **cells**, tiny living units, work together to make an organised body.

More cells alone wouldn't solve it. A larger heap is still a heap.

## 🧬 The starting parcel

Human development begins when a sperm joins an egg. Each normally contributes 23 **chromosomes**, packages of DNA.

**DNA**, or **deoxyribonucleic acid**, stores inherited information. Sections called **genes** help cells make working molecules, including proteins used for construction and communication.

The egg brings much more than information. It supplies nearly all the starting cell's contents: materials, working machinery and stored molecules that help early development begin.

So development starts with an already living cell. DNA isn't sitting alone on a table trying to assemble a baby.

## 🫧 Dividing without immediately getting bigger

At first, the cell divides into smaller cells without a similar increase in the whole embryo's size. Picture cutting a ball of dough into pieces: there are more pieces, but not more dough.

The comparison stops there, because the cells actively organise themselves. They gather closely, then form a structure with a fluid-filled space. Some cells are on the outside; others form an inner group.

Those positions matter. Different cells receive different signals and begin different jobs. Some contribute to the embryo itself, while others help make supporting structures.

The developing structure attaches to the lining of the **uterus**, or womb, and becomes embedded there.

## 🧭 How can a cell know where it is?

Cells release chemical signals that other cells can detect. A nearby cell may receive more of a signal than a distant one. It also responds according to its earlier history and what other signals are present.

Those signals influence which genes it uses. This helps cells specialise, becoming different kinds of tissue.

Cells also move, stick together and pull on one another. Sheets of cells can bend and fold into tubes. Development is a building process involving both chemistry and physical forces.

Early cells reorganise into three broad layers. Their descendants contribute to different parts: outer skin and nervous tissue from one, muscles and blood from another, and much of the gut lining from a third. Whole organs usually combine several tissue types.

## ✋ Sometimes building means removing

Growing a hand involves adding cells, shaping tissues and removing some cells between the developing fingers.

This controlled cell death helps separate structures. Similar processes remove cells that are no longer needed elsewhere in the body.

The same chemical signals can be used for different tasks in different places. Their effects depend on timing and the cells receiving them. Development gets remarkable variety from a set of tools used in many combinations.

## 🌿 How does the growing body get supplies?

The **placenta** is an organ that supports exchange between the mother and developing baby. Oxygen and nutrients cross towards the baby; wastes cross back for removal.

Their blood normally stays in separate circulations, with materials moving across the tissues between them. The placenta also makes chemical signals and helps manage the relationship between the two bodies.

## 👯 What twins reveal

Fraternal twins begin from two fertilised eggs. Identical twins begin from one, with development producing two embryos.

Even identical twins need not become identical people. Later biological changes and different experiences matter. The name describes their shared beginning, not a promise that every detail will match.

Scientists understand many of the local steps, but can't yet predict the entire construction of a human body from its starting conditions. [Gene regulation](../genetics-and-evolution/gene-expression-and-regulation.md) explains some of the instructions-in-use. The remarkable result is a body whose builders are also its building materials.
