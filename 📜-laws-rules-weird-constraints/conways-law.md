# Conway's Law: Your Org Chart Has Entered The Architecture 📜

## The Law

Conway's Law says that organizations tend to design systems that mirror their communication structures.

The short version: the software looks like the team that made it.

## Why It Bites

If three teams barely talk, do not be shocked when the product has three awkwardly joined parts. If every decision climbs five management ladders, do not be shocked when the software feels like bureaucracy with buttons.

Architecture diagrams often pretend to describe technology. Conway's Law asks whether they are actually sociology diagrams in a nice font.

## Useful Questions

- Which teams need to talk for this system to feel coherent?
- Where do handoffs create weird product seams?
- Does the communication path match the desired architecture?
- Are we asking one team structure to produce a different system structure by sheer optimism?

## Why It Is Interesting

The law is useful because it moves design out of the codebase and into the room. Sometimes the fix is not a new framework. Sometimes the fix is changing who can talk to whom without scheduling a ceremony.
