# How Cells Use and Regulate Genes 📖

A neuron and a liver cell contain largely the same nuclear DNA. Their different jobs depend on which genes they use, how much product they make and how those products interact.

**Gene expression** is the process of using genetic information to make functional RNA or protein. **Regulation** controls that process. A genome contains possibilities; a cell's state determines which possibilities become active.

## 🧬 From DNA to RNA to protein

During **transcription**, RNA polymerase uses one DNA strand as a template to make RNA. For a protein-coding gene, the resulting messenger RNA, or mRNA, carries a sequence that can be translated by a ribosome.

In eukaryotes, newly made mRNA usually undergoes processing. A cap and a poly-A tail contribute to stability, transport and translation. **Splicing** removes introns and joins exons. Alternative splicing can produce different transcripts from one gene, although not every detected transcript necessarily makes a useful protein.

During **translation**, the ribosome reads mRNA in three-base units called **codons**. Transfer RNAs pair with codons and deliver amino acids. Enzymes called aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases load the correct amino acids onto those tRNAs. The ribosome joins the amino acids into a growing chain.

The ribosome's catalytic centre is RNA. RNA therefore carries information and participates directly in the machinery that interprets it. This is one reason RNA features so prominently in [origin-of-life research](../open-questions-in-biology.md).

The usual flow is DNA → RNA → protein, but RNA can also be copied into DNA by reverse transcriptases. This does not contradict the precise central-dogma claim that sequence information does not flow back out of protein into nucleic acid. The simplified classroom arrow lost a few footnotes.

## 🎛️ Regulation happens at several stages

A cell can change a gene's output by controlling:

- How accessible its DNA is within chromatin.
- Whether transcription factors and RNA polymerase initiate transcription.
- How an RNA is spliced, transported or degraded.
- How efficiently an mRNA is translated.
- Whether the resulting protein is modified, activated, moved or destroyed.

**Promoters** help specify where transcription begins. **Enhancers** are regulatory DNA regions that can influence transcription from a distance, often through three-dimensional contacts. Their effects depend on cell type and the proteins present.

These controls form networks. One transcription factor may activate another, which then reinforces the original state or inhibits an alternative. Feedback helps a differentiated cell preserve its identity while remaining responsive to signals.

Not every gene is either fully on or fully off. Amount, timing and location can be as consequential as presence. Development depends on those distinctions; [embryonic development](../bodies-and-energy/embryonic-development.md) follows how local signals help build a whole body.

## 📝 Epigenetics without the family horoscope

Epigenetic mechanisms can maintain differences in gene activity without changing the underlying DNA sequence. DNA methylation, histone modifications and associated regulatory machinery contribute to this cellular memory.

“Heritable” often means passed from one cell to its daughter cells. It does not automatically mean passed from a grandparent to a grandchild. Much epigenetic information is reset during mammalian reproduction and early development.

Some environmental exposures affect descendants, but separating inherited marks from shared environments, direct fetal exposure and genetic differences is difficult. Evidence for transgenerational inheritance varies greatly among organisms; it is much easier to demonstrate in some plants and invertebrates than to establish for broad human traits. [Heard and Martienssen's review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4020004/) explains why these distinctions matter.

**Genomic imprinting** is a well-established special case: certain genes are expressed differently depending on which parent supplied them. The relevant marks are established and reset in a regulated reproductive cycle. It is a specific mechanism, not evidence that every experience is written into future generations.

## 🐙 RNA can be edited too

RNA processing can alter more than which segments remain. In **A-to-I editing**, enzymes convert adenosine to inosine, which cellular machinery often reads like guanosine. Some edits therefore change the amino acid specified by an RNA without changing the DNA.

Coleoid cephalopods, including octopuses and squid, have unusually extensive protein-recoding RNA editing. A [2017 study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5499236/) examined the relationship between that editing and constraints on surrounding DNA sequences.

This is a striking form of molecular flexibility. It does not establish that editing explains octopus intelligence or that an octopus consciously rewrites its genes. The interesting mechanism is already strange enough without giving it editorial meetings.

## 🦠 Genes can move between lineages

**Horizontal gene transfer** moves genetic material between organisms outside ordinary parent-to-offspring inheritance. Bacteria can acquire environmental DNA, exchange plasmids or receive DNA carried by viruses. These routes can spread traits such as antibiotic resistance.

The history of a particular gene can therefore differ from the history of the organism carrying it. Microbial evolution contains branching descent and exchange; both matter.

CRISPR systems illustrate a different interaction with foreign DNA. Some bacteria and archaea retain short sequences from invading genetic elements. RNAs derived from these stored sequences guide associated proteins to matching targets. Different systems target DNA or RNA.

The gene-editing tools developed from CRISPR exploit this sequence recognition. Its original biological role was defence, demonstrated experimentally in bacteria before it became a laboratory technology. [Microbes](../microbes.md) and [immunity](../bodies-and-energy/immunity.md) explore the larger context.

## 🔄 Copying cells and making gametes

In **mitosis**, replicated chromosomes are separated into daughter nuclei. It supports growth and tissue renewal while generally maintaining chromosome number.

In **meiosis**, homologous chromosomes pair, exchange DNA and then separate through two divisions. The resulting cells have one chromosome set. Recombination and independent assortment generate new combinations of inherited variants.

Neither process makes mutation unnecessary. Recombination rearranges existing variants; mutation creates new sequence variants. Somatic mutations can influence cell-lineage evolution within a body, including cancer. Mutations transmitted through reproductive lineages can affect later generations.

These distinctions connect gene regulation to [DNA structure](dna-and-chromosomes.md), [family resemblance](inheritance.md) and [evolution](evolution.md).
