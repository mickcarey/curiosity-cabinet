# Reading The Bush 🌲

*A south-east Australian field companion: tree → soil → fire → water → what could actually live here*

This is the outside voice of [The Living World](../🌱-the-living-world/README.md).

That series teaches the method: climate, rock, soil, water, plants, disturbance. This shelf takes the method for a walk. Victoria first. The Sunshine Coast as the second dialect. The rest of Australia as "same song, different key."

I am still the AI narrator. Mick is still the human who wanted to walk into a forest and stop treating it like a green screensaver. The dream is simple and slightly arrogant: see a tree, infer the soil, guess what else could grow here, and know which part of that guess is solid versus swagger.

The science lives next door. Chapter 14 of the living-world series is the global land method. These notes are the local application. Do not wait for every science chapter before going outside. The bush will not mark your homework. It will just keep being itself.

## How To Use This 🧭

Look. Infer. Then check.

1. **Notice the obvious** — wet or dry, open or closed, ferns or grass, sand or clay on your boots.
2. **Name the kind, not the species** — stringybark country is a sentence. "I think that might be a messmate" is a bonus round.
3. **Ask what it implies** — moisture, fire, soil fertility, salt, wind, time since the last burn.
4. **Ask what else would belong** — and what would die here looking surprised.
5. **Check later** — a book, a key, a ranger sign, a photograph. Inference is a first draft.

This is literacy, not a licence. "Could grow here" is not "should plant here," and it is definitely not "the council will thank you."

## Home Range 🇦🇺

- **Victoria first** — Otways, Dandenongs, box-ironbark, coastal tea-tree, the dry hills that make Melbourne's wet gullies look like they are showing off
- **Sunshine Coast as the warmer dialect** — rainforest edges, wallum, that sudden subtropical humidity that makes Victorian sclerophyll look like it has been on a diet
- **Portable method** — if it works on a walk from Keilor Downs, it should still work when the trees change their surnames

## What This Is Not 🚫

- Not a complete Australian flora. There are hundreds of eucalypts. We need the *types*.
- Not a foraging guide. No "this mushroom is fine." No "the bush is a pantry if you are brave." The bush is a living system. Dinner is a supermarket problem.
- Not a gardening catalogue, though chapter 06 is allowed to be useful to a gardener who can handle bad news.
- Not a replacement for the [fungal network](../✈️-airplane-reading/the-fungal-network-beneath-our-feet.md) note. That one is the underground internet. This one is the landscape the internet is installed in.

If you cannot tell a stringybark from a banksia, you are in the right place. If you already can, stay anyway. The trick is not the name. The trick is the implication.

## The Trail (chapters still in the pack)

The trail is built. Method first, then the walk.

**[01. The climate and the rock](01-the-climate-and-the-rock.md)**
Why the Otways are not the You Yangs. Rainfall gradients you can feel in the windscreen. Geology you can actually see: sandstone, basalt, granite, the coastal limestone that makes tea-tree look smug. Climate and parent rock are the first two cards. Plants are the comment they leave in the margin.

**[02. Soils you can see](02-soils-you-can-see.md)**
Sand, clay, loam, laterite, peaty wet forest, coastal alkaline sand, wallum. Colour. Texture-by-feel. Drainage. pH clues from the plants, which are ruder and more honest than a test kit you left in the car. Soil is not dirt. Dirt is what you call soil when you have not been introduced.

**[03. Eucalypts as a reading key](03-eucalypts-as-a-reading-key.md)**
The practical superpower of this continent. Stringybark, peppermint, gum, ironbark, box: a moisture-fire-soil codebook wearing bark. You do not need all eight hundred species. You need to know what *kind* of eucalypt country you have walked into, the way a guitarist needs to know the key, not every possible chord.

**[04. The supporting cast](04-the-supporting-cast.md)**
Acacia, banksia, casuarina, tea-tree. Rainforest taxa versus sclerophyll. Fern gullies as a water story with extra theatre. Grass trees as fire-and-time wearing a skirt. If eucalypts are the headline, this chapter is everyone else who makes the scene make sense.

**[05. Fire, water, and succession](05-fire-water-and-succession.md)**
Why so much of Australia looks like this and not like a postcard of England having a nervous breakdown. Fire regimes. Resprouters versus seeders. Wet gully refuges. What a post-fire walk is telling you, besides "that was a lot of smoke." Climax forest is a bit of a myth. The bush is a conversation that keeps getting interrupted.

**[06. Therefore you could grow this](06-therefore-you-could-grow-this.md)**
The horticultural inference engine. If the remnant vegetation is X, the soil and water are probably Y, so these plants are *plausible*. Also: why imported garden dreams die here looking personally betrayed, and why "the soil is wrong" is often "the fire, the water, or the fungi are wrong." Could ≠ should. The bush is not a mood board.

**[07. Walkable case studies](07-walkable-case-studies.md)**
Worked examples, not a rewrite of the hike guide:

- Otways / fern gully
- Dandenongs wet sclerophyll
- Coastal tea-tree on calcareous sand
- Dry box-ironbark
- Sunshine Coast rainforest edge / wallum

Each case: what you see → what it implies → what would *not* belong. The last one is the test. Anyone can say "nice trees." Literacy is knowing the absences.

## A Tiny Taste Of The Method 🔍

You do not need chapter 03 to start. Try this on the next walk, then come back and laugh at your first draft.

| You notice | A decent first inference | A bad leap |
|---|---|---|
| Tall wet forest, tree ferns, the air feels rinsed | Reliable moisture, shelter, fire is a visitor not a landlord | "This is rainforest, so tropical plants would love my backyard" |
| Ironbark and tough grass, light coming through everywhere | Drier, harder, fire is part of the furniture | "Nothing grows here" (things grow here, they just do not grow *soft*) |
| Banksias and tea-tree on pale sand | Low nutrient, maybe coastal, maybe acid, drainage is the whole personality | "I should add lots of fertiliser and a maple" |
| Grass trees and charcoal stories in the bark | Fire has been here, and the plants have opinions about the interval | "Fire is gone now, the bush is finished" |

Inference is a first draft. Check it. The bush is patient. Your certainty does not have to be.
