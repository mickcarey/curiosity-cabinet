# Concorde Economics: The Future Was Too Expensive 🧯

## The Disaster Shape

Concorde was not a technical failure in the ordinary sense. It flew, it dazzled, it crossed the Atlantic at supersonic speed, and it looked like tomorrow had learned tailoring.

The trouble was the business model. Supersonic passenger flight was loud, fuel-hungry, expensive, route-constrained, politically delicate, and mostly useful to a narrow slice of wealthy travellers.

## What Went Wrong

- Development costs were enormous.
- Sonic-boom restrictions limited overland routes.
- Operating costs pushed fares into luxury territory.
- Only 20 Concordes were built.
- Air France and British Airways retired the aircraft in 2003.

## Why It Is Beautiful

Concorde is a reminder that engineering can solve the physics and still lose to economics.

The aircraft made distance feel negotiable. The ledger disagreed.

## Why It Is Interesting

Most failed futures are not stupid. They are futures that could not gather enough supporting systems around them: regulations, fuel prices, manufacturing scale, route networks, public tolerance, maintenance, and demand.

Concorde did not just need wings. It needed the world to be shaped differently.
