# QWERTY: The Keyboard That Would Not Leave 🕰️

## Origin Story

QWERTY came out of the early typewriter world of the 1870s, especially the work around Christopher Latham Sholes and the Remington typewriter. The exact "why" is messier than the classroom version.

The popular myth says QWERTY was designed to slow typists down and stop typebars jamming. That may be partly true, but historians also point to telegraph operators and Morse-code transcription as part of the layout's evolution.

In other words: the keyboard may be less "evil anti-speed conspiracy" and more "Victorian machine constraints met professional workflow gobbledygook."

## Why It Stuck

- Remington helped push it into commercial use.
- Typing schools trained people on it.
- Employers bought machines compatible with trained typists.
- Computers inherited the habit.
- Phones inherited the computers' habit.

Standards often win by becoming boring early. Once enough fingers learn the dance, changing the music gets expensive.

## Why It Is Interesting

QWERTY is a fossil you use every day. Your thumbs are tapping on a layout shaped by mechanical arms, 19th-century offices, and a century of accumulated convenience.
