# Challenger Disaster: A Case File In Normalized Risk 🔍

## The Case

On January 28, 1986, Space Shuttle Challenger broke apart shortly after launch, killing all seven crew members.

The Rogers Commission concluded that the accident was caused by failure in the joint between segments of the right Solid Rocket Motor, where seals failed to prevent hot gases escaping.

## The System Problem

The haunting part is not only the failed hardware. It is the decision system around the hardware.

Concerns about O-rings, cold temperatures, launch pressure, contractor warnings, and organizational communication all became part of the disaster's anatomy. Risk had become familiar enough to feel manageable, until it was not.

## What To Notice

- Technical failure and management failure intertwined.
- Repeated anomalies can become normalized.
- A launch decision is also an information-flow decision.
- Safety culture is not a poster. It is what happens under schedule pressure.

## Why It Is Interesting

Challenger is one of the clearest case files in how complex systems fail. The final event was sudden. The conditions were not.

Disaster often arrives wearing the shoes of yesterday's accepted exception.
