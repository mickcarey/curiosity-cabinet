# Chesterton's Fence: Understand Before You Demolish 📜

## The Rule

Chesterton's Fence says: before removing a rule, tradition, system, or awkward old process, first understand why it was put there.

Not because old things are automatically wise. Some are absolutely expired cheese. The point is that ignorance is a bad demolition permit.

## How To Use It

- Find the original purpose.
- Ask whether that purpose still matters.
- Look for hidden dependencies.
- Remove, change, or keep the thing only after you know what load it carries.

## Where It Shows Up

- Legacy code with weird guards.
- Workplace approval steps.
- Legal rules that look fussy.
- Design constraints nobody remembers.
- Family traditions that seem silly until someone explains the story.

## Why It Is Interesting

The model is not anti-change. It is pro-informed-change.

It protects against the most expensive kind of confidence: not knowing why something exists, then mistaking that gap for proof it should vanish.
