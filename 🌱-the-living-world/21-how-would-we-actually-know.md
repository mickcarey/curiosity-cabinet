# How Would We Actually Know? ⚖️

Finding life would not look like a movie. It would look like a committee.

Committees are unfashionable. They are also how you keep a civilisation from declaring a dust cloud a neighbour, then getting bored when the dust remains dust. Patience is not a vibe. It is a method.

A stain in a rock. A gas in a spectrum. A seasonal wiggle. A sample-return argument that lasts fifty years. Little green men are a cartoon of a weirder possibility: that the process from [chapter 01](01-what-even-is-life.md) is running somewhere we cannot poke, and that the first good evidence will be ambiguous on purpose, because the universe did not sit the exam.

Sentences are cheap. Certifying a microbe is not. This is the last climb of the series, and it is mostly a lesson in not fooling ourselves. [Chapter 19](19-life-as-an-astrobiology-problem.md) was the life question. [Chapter 20](20-other-worlds-worth-checking.md) was the suspect list. This page is the courtroom.

We might find it and argue for a generation. That would still be the most interesting argument in the building.

If you wanted a flying saucer, this is the wrong series. If you wanted a method, you are in the last room, and the room is a courtroom with a tide chart on the wall.

The tide chart is not a joke. We learned to read water before we learned to read a spectrum. That was the point of bringing the ocean along.

A barnacle line is a detection that already cleared the ladder: multiple independent organisms, no abiotic path that builds a city of calcium at high water, reproducible tomorrow.

Earth is the calibration target. We should not forget we already passed.

## The Detection Problem 🔍

You are looking for a process you cannot close, using instruments that have Earth on them, in environments that do chemistry whether or not anyone is home.

Four ways to get this wrong, before we get to famous wrecks:

1. **Contamination.** We brought the bug. Ryugu samples grew terrestrial rods in a lab that had tried to be clean. Spacecraft sit in clean rooms that are not actually sterile. A Mars microbe that sequences as *Bacillus* is a travel story.
2. **Abiotic mimicry.** Rocks make organics. Photochemistry makes gases. Shock makes magnetite. Volcanoes make almost everything a press release has ever loved.
3. **Instrument and model error.** A spectral line that is also another molecule's line. A retrieval that prefers dimethyl sulfide because you asked it to. A calibration that shifted in the night.
4. **Imagination.** We want it. Want is not a method, but it will sit in the room and look helpful.

A good detection is not "a thing that would be life if it were life." It is a thing that has survived those four, in public, more than once, with an abiotic path that had a fair chance to win and lost.

Viking is the original scar. In 1976 the labeled-release experiment fed radioactive nutrients to Martian soil and got radioactive gas back, heat-sterilisable, at both landing sites. Gilbert Levin went to his grave sure it was metabolism.

The gas chromatograph did not find organics at the expected level, the other biology boxes disagreed, and perchlorate chemistry later offered abiotic ways to chew the same lunch. Available facts, as the first paper said, did not permit a conclusion. Fifty years later they still do not, not cleanly.

That is not a reason to sneer at Viking. It is a reason to build the ladder. A mission can be both historic and inconclusive. Most of the interesting ones will be.

## False Positives We Already Own 🧯

### ALH84001: The Fossil That Was A Fight

1996. David McKay, Everett Gibson, Kathie Thomas-Keprta, and colleagues, in *Science*: carbonate globules in a Martian meteorite from Allan Hills, possible nanofossils, organics, magnetite crystals that looked like the little bar magnets magnetotactic bacteria chain up.

President Clinton gave a statement. Astrobiology, as a funded sport, got a push it is still spending.

Then the grind. Nanofossil shapes turned out to be easy for geology. Organics can be water-rock, or Earth contamination in an Antarctic wait. Magnetite — the best remaining pillar — can be made by decomposing carbonate in a shock.

Some of the original team still argue a subpopulation matches bacterial magnetosomes too well. Most of the field treats ALH84001 as **not a detection**. Andrew Steele's later work found abiotic organic synthesis in the rock and called the polarisation eased.

The useful residue: **four lines of evidence that all have abiotic stories are not four lines of evidence.** They are one rock having a complicated day.

Also: extraordinary claims are allowed. They pay rent in the form of other people trying to kill them. That is the method working, not the method failing. Sagan's line about extraordinary evidence is a slogan. The working version is: extraordinary claims get a longer ladder, not a gag.

### Venus Phosphine: The Line That Became A Plumbing Problem

2020. Greaves et al., phosphine in Venus's clouds, unexplained, carefully footnoted, immediately un-footnoted by the internet.

Reanalyses: maybe SO₂, maybe the ALMA pipeline, maybe the wrong altitude, maybe a real leftover of unknown chemistry that still is not a microbe. [Chapter 20](20-other-worlds-worth-checking.md) told the story. Here the moral is colder.

A single transition, at a resolution that cannot separate neighbours, in an atmosphere that is a sulfuric-acid crime scene, is not a biosphere. It is a **candidate spectral feature**.

Candidate features deserve papers. They do not deserve "life on Venus" as a headline, and they do not deserve to be memory-holed when they wobble.

The hangover is part of the data. So is the original team's right to defend the line. Science is not a press cycle. It is a series of revisions that should be allowed to feel embarrassing.

### Tabby's Star: The Megastructure Temptation

[Tabby's Star](../✈️-airplane-reading/tabbys-star.md) dimmed too deep, too irregular, too unlike a planet. Citizen scientists caught it in Kepler data. The spicy hypothesis was an alien solar farm.

Follow-up found wavelength-dependent dimming: dust, not an umbrella. A 2026 paper even dangled a giant companion that might be stirring the debris. Less cinematic. Still a system with a past.

The moral is not "never think of technosignatures." The moral is **ordinary physics gets the first shot, and a Dyson sphere is not ordinary physics.** Dust is not boring. Dust is how most astrophysical mysteries pay the rent. If you skip dust and go to megastructures, you are not being open-minded. You are being impatient with a guitar that is still in the case.

### Honourable Mentions, Briefly

Methane on Mars: detected, not detected, seasonal, a rover sniff, a leak in the lab, a serpentinization maybe. A soap opera, not a verdict. K2-18b's dimethyl sulfide: a JWST retrieval at about 3 sigma, other molecules fitting as well, independent teams unimpressed. GFAJ-1 "arsenic life": contamination of a method, useful as a scar. Canal-building Martians: Percival Lowell, bad seeing, a warning from the 19th century that we are still capable of drawing canals in a spectrum.

False *negatives* deserve a seat. Earth with life and no obvious oxygen for a couple of billion years. A biosphere under ice with nothing in the spectrum. A metabolism that does not make our favourite gases. A sample that got sterilised by the method, which is the cruel joke of Viking's ovens and of any protocol that bakes before it looks. We can miss a neighbour by being too sure what a neighbour smells like.

The pattern is almost affectionate. We are good at noticing. We are too good at completing the picture. We are also good at designing tests that would not notice a different picture. Humility has to face both ways.

## The Ladder Of Claims 🪜

NASA has tried, twice, to make the staircase visible so that "hint" and "we are not alone" stop being used as synonyms.

Marc Neveu and colleagues, 2018: the **Ladder of Life Detection**. Features of life as we know it, ordered roughly from "suspicious biomaterial" to "I will eat my hat," with criteria: sensitive, clean, repeatable, preserved, different from the abiotic background, compatible with life, and — the grown-up one — **biology as the last-resort hypothesis**.

James Green and colleagues, 2021, in *Nature*: the **CoLD scale**, Confidence of Life Detection, seven rungs for talking in public without lying.

1. **Detect a possible signal.** A molecule, a texture, a gas. Cheyava Falls lives here. So did phosphine, on a good day.
2. **Rule out contamination.** Is this us? Is this the spacecraft? Is this the lab?
3. **Show the environment could host the biology you are implying.** Analogues, energy, solvent, not a fantasy in a vacuum.
4. **Rule out known non-biology.** The abiotic paths had a fair fight and lost. This is the rung almost nobody has honestly cleared off Earth.
5. **An independent second signal.** Different instrument, different molecule, different morphology, same story.
6. **Beat the new alternatives** that appear after the first paper, because they will.
7. **Independent confirmation.** Other team, other lab, other spacecraft, preferably another world-watching civilisation's equivalent of a bad mood. We have never been here.

Nothing claimed so far sits happily above 1 or 2. Viking, ALH84001, Venus, K2-18b, methane on Mars: the arguments are about whether they deserve 1, and why they fail 4. That is not pessimism. That is the scale doing the job of a scale.

This series' plainer ladder, if you hate acronyms:

**Interesting chemistry → possible biosignature → probably biology → we are not alone.**

Each arrow is a year, or ten, or fifty. Do not take two arrows at a bound because a press officer has a deadline. The arrows are the work. The last phrase is a consequence, not a brand.

## What A Good Detection Looks Like 🎯

Not a vibe. A checklist you can fail.

- **More than one independent line.** A gas *and* a season *and* a rock texture *and* an isotope ratio, not four photographs of the same rumour.
- **No known abiotic path that still fits.** Known is doing work. Unknown chemistry will be offered. It has to be worse than the biology, not merely possible in a footnote.
- **Reproducible.** Another instrument, another team, another transit, another sample. A one-off is a ghost story.
- **Contamination budgeted and beaten.** Blanks, controls, the Viking heat-sterilised twin, the clean-room map.
- **Context that makes sense.** Oxygen with methane on a world that could hold liquid water is ruder, and better, than oxygen alone on a desiccated rock around an ultraviolet dragon.
- **Biology as last resort, then still standing.** Neveu's rung. You do not get to start there.
- **A prediction that could have failed.** Daisyworld earned Gaia a little credit by being a model. A biosignature claim should say what would kill it. If nothing would kill it, it is not a claim. It is a mascot.

Sample return is the luxury version of this list. A Mars rock in a lab can be shared, restocked, re-analysed by enemies. A JWST wiggle cannot. That is why exoplanet detections will argue longer, and why they still have to be done. **fl**, the life fraction, will not come from one kitchen. It will come from a census of wiggles that survived the list.

Press versus papers is part of the method now, whether scientists like it or not. A university release will say "strongest hints yet." The paper will say 3 sigma and a maybe. CoLD was invented, in part, so that journalists and agencies had a staircase instead of a light switch. Use it. If a headline skips from rung 1 to "we are not alone," the correction is not pedantry. It is planetary protection for the public brain.

Sports: a good detection is not a wonder-goal from thirty yards that the replay invalidates. It is a 3–0 win with the second goal on video, the third from a different camera, and the other team admitting the offside trap failed. Beautiful. Slow. Repeatable. The crowd can still chant. The chant is not the score.

## SETI Versus Biosignature Science 📻

Do both. Do not confuse them.

**Biosignature science** hunts the stain. Microbes, metabolisms, disequilibria, possibly four billion years of quiet. False positives are geological and photochemical. The signal may be globally obvious (our oxygen) or shy (Archaean Earth). JWST, Clipper, Dragonfly, sample return.

**SETI** hunts the radio, the laser, the industrial NO₂, the megastructure that is not dust. False positives are pulsars (been there), dust (Tabby), calibration (been there too). A genuine narrow-band beacon would skip several CoLD rungs because geology does not beep on one frequency for fun. The catch is the assumption: that anyone is beeping, now, at us, on a channel we funded.

Technosignatures are the Fermi question's evidence. Biosignatures are the life question's evidence. A planet can fail SETI for four billion years and still be a biosphere. A SETI hit without a biosignature would be the strangest paper of all — a civilisation on a world that does not look alive, or a probe, or a lie we have not named yet.

The Wow! signal of 1977 is SETI's ALH84001: a seventy-two-second "hello?" that never repeated. It was not a detection. It was a homework problem that got a nickname. Repeating, narrow-band, extra-terrestrial, and inconsistent with geology: that is the SETI version of the good-detection list. One pulse is a ghost. A beacon that sits there while we argue is a neighbour. We have the ghost. We do not have the neighbour. That is allowed to be a result and a reason to leave the receiver on.

The cheapness of SETI is an argument for doing it, not for letting it set the meaning of "search for life." Look for the stain. Keep the radio on. If Tabby's Star taught the public that astronomers "almost found aliens," the correction is part of the education. Mystery means dust often enough that dust should get the headline it earned.

## We Might Argue For A Generation 🕰️

Assume, tomorrow, Clipper tastes a plume with a cocktail of organics, disequilibrium, and a chirality bias. Assume Perseverance's tubes land, and the leopard spots are full of textures that look grown, with isotopes that metabolism likes, and no lab on Earth can cook the abiotic replica. Assume JWST's successor sees oxygen and methane in a context Meadows cannot kill.

What happens is not a parade. What happens is:

- a year of "extraordinary"
- a decade of "yes, but the unknown photochemistry"
- a conference circuit of analogue sites
- a student who was not born at the announcement writing the paper that kills or crowns it
- a public that already moved on, or never stopped believing in canals

ALH84001 has been arguing since 1996. Viking since 1976. That is not a glitch. That is what a detection problem with a sample size of one looks like when the sample might have just become two. A generation of argument, if the argument is honest, *is* the knowing. Certainty is a movie. Science is a long season, and the away games matter.

Earth analogues of this patience are worth stealing. The ozone hole was a fight. Continental drift was a fight. Endosymbiosis was a fight Margulis had to win ugly. The good detections were the ones that accumulated independent lines until the abiotic (or the conservative) story cost too much. Nobody got a parade on day one. The parade, if you want one, is the textbooks changing.

That is actually the hopeful version. We do not need a face in a visor. We need a process that survives the ladder. If it takes fifty years to agree, we will have spent fifty years becoming the sort of species that can agree. That is not nothing. That is the other three bills — energy, information, structure — being paid by a civilisation that decided the stain was worth the patience.

Worked example, because the ladder is abstract until you climb it with a wet moon.

Suppose a future Enceladus fly-through finds: (a) amino acids with a left-handed excess, (b) H₂ still pouring, (c) lipids that look like membranes, (d) no Earth-bug sequences in the blanks. That is a beautiful morning. It is still, on CoLD, a climb: contamination (d) is rung 2; the kitchen (b) is rung 3; the abiotic amino-acid problem is rung 4, because meteorites also carry a slight L-excess; (a) plus (c) is the independent second line of rung 5. You do not get to say "we are not alone" at the press conference. You get to say "we have a sample that just made the abiotic story expensive, and we will fly again." If the second flight finds the same chirality and a polymer that copies, the committee's voice changes. If the second flight finds a racemic mix and a calibration story, you just lived Venus phosphine in a colder key. Both outcomes are science. Only one of them is a neighbour.

And if the ladder stays empty? That is a result. **fl** might be small. Life might be a fluke, or a shadow we still have not designed a search for. The [Fermi paradox](../💡-concepts/fermi-paradox.md) would lean toward rare or filtered. The ocean would still be most of the living space we have. The barnacle line would still be a tide table. The unsolved list from [chapter 16](16-mysteries-theories-and-the-unsolved.md) would still be the honest centre of biology. We would not have wasted the climb. We would have learned how to look, which is the adult form of wonder.

## Still Unsolved 🕳️

- **Whether we have already seen it and misfiled it.** Viking, ALH84001, a shadow biosphere, a spectrum sitting in an archive. Unlikely for the famous cases. Not a law.
- **How high a wiggle must climb before we say the sentence.** CoLD is a proposal, not a court. The community will improvise under pressure. That is a social unsolved with scientific stakes.
- **Contamination in a solar system we have already sneezed on.** Forward contamination is not a sci-fi plot. It is a planetary-protection spreadsheet.
- **Unknown unknowns in abiotic chemistry.** Every time we say "no known abiotic path," a graduate student hears a thesis title.
- **How to tell a second genesis from a cousin.** Same handshake might mean panspermia, or destiny, or that we contaminated the sample. Opposite handshake would be the cleanest "not us" we could hope for, and we might still argue.
- **Whether a generation of argument is something a culture can still do.** That one is not in the methods section. It might be the filter.
- **How to keep SETI and biosignatures from stealing each other's headlines.** A beep is not a stain. A stain is not a city. The public brain will mash them anyway. The papers should not.
