# Carbohydrates and Fats: Fuel and Structure 🧱

You can eat toast. You cannot get the same nourishment by chewing the wooden table beneath it. Yet the starch in bread and much of the material in wood are built from the same small sugar: **glucose**. The connections make the difference.

**Carbohydrates** are sugars and molecules built from sugar units. **Fats** belong to a wider group called **lipids**, whose members tend to mix poorly with water. Both groups supply energy and building materials. They make structures as different as a leaf, a cell's outer boundary and a lobster's shell. The nutrition panel has seriously undersold their careers.

A **molecule** is a group of connected atoms. A **cell** is a tiny living unit enclosed by a selective boundary called a membrane. Change how those atoms connect and you can change what the molecule does—even when the ingredients remain the same.

## 🍬 Why starch is digestible and cellulose is not

Carbohydrates include simple sugars and chains assembled from them. Glucose, fructose and galactose are single sugar units, or **monosaccharides**. Table sugar, sucrose, joins two units. **Polysaccharides** are long chains of many units; the name literally tells you there are lots of sugars.

Both starch and cellulose are built from glucose. The difference lies in the bonds connecting the glucose units. Our digestive **enzymes**, molecules that speed up chemical reactions, can cut some connections but not others. The technical labels describe exactly which carbon atoms join and their orientation:

- Starch has mainly alpha-1,4 linkages, with alpha-1,6 branch points in amylopectin.
- Cellulose has beta-1,4 linkages, producing straight chains that assemble into strong bundles called microfibrils.

Human digestive enzymes can break the relevant bonds in starch, but we lack the cellulases needed to digest cellulose directly. Other organisms make these enzymes or depend on microbial partners that do. A cow's ability to use grass owes a great deal to organisms smaller than the cow has probably considered.

Cellulose is one major component of plant walls, alongside other sugar chains and, in wood, a strengthening material called **lignin**. **Chitin**, built from modified sugars, supports fungal walls and the outer skeletons of animals such as insects and crabs. Carbohydrate chemistry contributes to both leaves and lobsters.

## 🌰 Storing glucose without filling a cell with syrup

Plants store carbohydrate as **starch**, a mixture of relatively linear amylose and branched amylopectin. Animals store it as **glycogen**, which is more densely branched.

Branching supplies many ends where enzymes can add or remove glucose units. Joining sugars into long chains, or **polymers**, also helps control water movement. A large number of separate dissolved particles would draw water into the cell through **osmosis**, the movement of water across a selective membrane towards the more concentrated solution. Packing glucose into fewer, larger molecules reduces that pull. A cell needs fuel reserves, not an invitation to swell like a water balloon.

Liver glycogen can support blood glucose between meals. Muscle glycogen primarily supplies the muscle containing it; muscle lacks the enzyme needed to release substantial free glucose directly into the blood.

Starch accessibility changes with processing. Heating starch with water can disrupt its granular structure, making it easier for enzymes to reach. Cooling can allow some chains to reassociate into less digestible forms. The amount depends on the food and conditions, not simply whether a potato spent the night in the fridge.

That connection between structure and digestion is developed in [food energy](../bodies-and-energy/calories-and-food-energy.md).

## 🏷️ Sugars on cell surfaces

Short carbohydrate chains called **glycans** attach to proteins and lipids on cell surfaces and elsewhere. **Proteins** are chains of smaller units called amino acids that perform much of a cell's work. Adding glycans can alter how proteins fold, how stable they are and how other molecules recognise them. Proteins called **lectins** recognise particular glycan structures.

ABO blood groups provide a concrete example: the A and B forms differ in terminal sugars added to a precursor structure by specific enzymes. The common inherited versions responsible for group O do not produce an active enzyme for either addition. The important distinction is molecular detail on a surface, rather than a different overall kind of blood cell.

Glycans are assembled through enzyme pathways, rather than copied directly from a template like DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid), the molecule carrying inherited information. Glycans' branching and variable linkages make them difficult to characterise. [Glycobiology](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK579918/) studies this additional layer of biological structure and recognition.

## 🛢️ Several very different kinds of lipid

“Lipid” covers chemically diverse molecules that tend to be hydrophobic or amphipathic, meaning they contain both water-compatible and water-avoiding regions.

| Class | Main structural feature | Some roles |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fatty acids | Chain of carbon and hydrogen with an acidic end | Fuel and components of other lipids |
| Triglycerides | Three fatty acids joined to a small molecule called glycerol | Concentrated energy storage |
| Phospholipids | Water-friendly head and oily tails | Membrane formation |
| Sterols | Fused ring structure | Membrane properties and chemical precursors |
| Waxes | Long molecules that form water-resistant coatings | Protective surface barriers |

Fat stores substantial energy per gram because of its chemical arrangement. It contains many carbon–hydrogen bonds and comparatively little oxygen. When cells break it down using oxygen, transferring its electrons—tiny charged particles involved in chemical bonds—releases more usable energy per gram than the corresponding breakdown of typical carbohydrate. Triglycerides also associate with little water, whereas glycogen stores carry considerable water with them. This makes fat effective for compact, longer-term energy storage.

## 🫧 Membranes assemble, then cells manage them

Many phospholipids form **bilayers**, double layers with water-friendly heads exposed and water-avoiding tails tucked inside. This double layer restricts charged particles, called **ions**, and many water-soluble molecules. Embedded proteins provide selective passages and pumps to control what crosses.

Fatty-acid composition influences packing. Some carbon atoms are joined by double bonds. A common arrangement, called **cis**, introduces a bend in the chain. Bent tails pack less tightly, helping the membrane remain fluid; shorter tails can have a similar effect. Cholesterol modifies packing, permeability and fluidity in animal membranes.

A membrane is not a uniform bag. Its two sides can have different compositions, proteins interact with particular lipid environments, and cells constantly remodel it. These differences let one membrane insulate a nerve fibre and another hold an imbalance of charged particles that helps make **ATP**, adenosine triphosphate, a molecule used to power cellular work.

The mechanics belong with [cells and organelles](cells-and-organelles.md); the gradient appears in [cellular energy](../bodies-and-energy/digestion-and-cellular-energy.md).

## 🧪 Structure does not prescribe a diet

Cholesterol is used to make some **hormones**, chemical signals carried around the body, and bile acids, which help digest fats. Humans can make cholesterol themselves. We can also make many fatty acids, but cannot make the essential fatty acids linoleic acid and alpha-linolenic acid from scratch.

These are distinct facts. A molecule being essential to cellular function does not mean more dietary intake is always beneficial, or that the body must obtain that molecule directly from food. Nutritional questions depend on synthesis, absorption, transport and regulation as well as chemical roles.

The useful biological lesson is broader: energy-bearing molecules can also be indispensable materials. [Plants](../plant-biology.md), [immunity](../bodies-and-energy/immunity.md) and [protein modification](proteins-and-folding.md) all make use of that versatility.
