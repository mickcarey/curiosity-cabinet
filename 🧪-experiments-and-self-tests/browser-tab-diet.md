# The Browser Tab Diet 🧪

## The Experiment

For one week, limit open browser tabs to a fixed number. Start with 9. Not zero, because we are curious people, not monks made of spreadsheet cells.

The goal is to find out what the tabs are doing emotionally. Are they tasks? Reminders? Anxiety bookmarks? Tiny guilt rectangles?

## The Rules

- Pick a tab limit.
- When opening a new tab over the limit, close or save one first.
- At the end of each day, write down the oldest surviving tab.
- If a tab survives three days, convert it into a note, task, bookmark, or deletion.

## What To Notice

- Which tabs are genuinely useful?
- Which are postponed decisions?
- Which are identity theatre: "I am the sort of person who will read this 9,000-word essay on urban drainage"?
- Does the limit make browsing calmer or just more annoying?

## Why It Is Interesting

Tabs pretend to be information management. Often they are actually emotional storage. This experiment opens the cupboard and checks what is rattling around in there.
