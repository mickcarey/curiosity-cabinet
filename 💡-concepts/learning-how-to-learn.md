# Learning How To Learn 🧠🛠️

## The Meta-Skill Hiding Under Every Skill

Learning how to learn is the skill underneath piano, football, programming, parenting, cooking, fitness, and the lifelong art of not losing your keys in increasingly creative locations.

The big mistake is treating learning as **exposure**:

- I watched the tutorial.
- I read the chapter.
- I attended the lesson.
- I listened to the coach.

Exposure is useful, but it is not the same as learning. Exposure is rain on the soil. Learning is what happens when roots actually grab on.

## The Learning Loop 🔁

Most durable learning follows a loop:

1. **Input:** encounter the idea, movement, pattern, or technique.
2. **Attempt:** try to produce it yourself.
3. **Feedback:** notice what happened versus what should have happened.
4. **Adjustment:** change one specific thing.
5. **Retrieval:** recall or perform it later without looking.
6. **Transfer:** use it in a slightly different situation.

That last step is where the magic lives. If you can only play the piano passage at home, slowly, alone, after three warm-up attempts, you have learned a hallway. If you can play it in tempo, after a mistake, on a different piano, while slightly nervous, you have learned a road network.

## Comprehension Is Not A Glow Feeling 💡

Comprehension often *feels* like fluency:

- The explanation makes sense.
- The teacher is clear.
- The video is smooth.
- The article has nice diagrams.

But fluency can be a trick mirror. Something can feel understood because the source is carrying the load for you.

Better tests of comprehension:

- Can I explain it without the source?
- Can I give a new example?
- Can I spot where it breaks?
- Can I compare it with a similar idea?
- Can I use it tomorrow, in a messier setting?

If the idea collapses when the textbook leaves the room, it was not internalized yet. It was just visiting.

## Internalising A Skill 🎹⚽

For skills, the question is not "Do I know what to do?" but "Can my body and attention do it under pressure?"

Piano:

- Slow practice builds clean movement.
- Hands-separate practice exposes weak wiring.
- Short loops beat heroic full-song flailing.
- Recording yourself removes the polite lies your ears tell in real time.

Football:

- Isolated drills build ingredients.
- Small-sided games build decisions.
- Weak-foot touches make the skill less fragile.
- Scanning before receiving the ball is often more valuable than one more fancy touch.

General life:

- Systems beat moods.
- Checklists beat heroic remembering.
- Weekly reviews beat vague anxiety soup.
- Calendar blocks beat "I'll find time," the famous last words of many good intentions.

## The Three Kinds Of Knowing 🧩

Useful learning usually needs all three:

- **Declarative knowing:** facts and concepts. "A major scale follows this interval pattern."
- **Procedural knowing:** actions and routines. "My fingers can play the scale smoothly."
- **Conditional knowing:** judgment. "This is when to use it, change it, or ignore it."

Many people stop at declarative knowing and wonder why real life still feels slippery. That is like reading a cookbook and being surprised the pasta did not boil itself.

## The Bedtime Version 🌙

Before sleeping, ask:

- What did I learn today?
- Where did I struggle?
- What is one small thing I would try differently next time?
- What example proves I actually understand it?

This gives your sleeping brain a tidy little bundle to chew on. Not a chaos buffet. A snack plate.

## The Tiny Practice Recipe

Use this for almost anything:

1. Pick one narrow target.
2. Do a short attempt.
3. Get feedback from reality.
4. Fix one thing.
5. Repeat a few times.
6. Stop before you become mush.
7. Revisit tomorrow.

Learning loves repetition, but it loves *informative* repetition most. Ten mindful reps beat a hundred zombie reps wearing a tracksuit.

## Connections

- **Memory palaces:** good for ordered recall, not a replacement for understanding.
- **Flow state:** enjoyable, but not always where the hardest improvement happens.
- **Dunning-Kruger effect:** early fluency can inflate confidence before skill is actually stable.
- **Sunk cost fallacy:** practice time does not count if the method is broken.
