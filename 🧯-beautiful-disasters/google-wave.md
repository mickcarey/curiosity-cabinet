# Google Wave: Email From A Timeline That Did Not Arrive 🧯

## The Disaster Shape

Google Wave tried to rethink communication as live, collaborative, replayable conversation. It mixed email, chat, documents, bots, rich media, and real-time editing into one ambitious web app.

The demo was dazzling. The product was harder to explain.

## What Went Wrong

Google announced in August 2010 that Wave had not reached the adoption it wanted and would not continue as a standalone product. The ideas were impressive, but the package asked users to understand a new category before they knew what problem it was solving.

Sometimes being early looks exactly like being confusing.

## What Survived

- Real-time collaboration became normal elsewhere.
- Character-by-character typing and collaborative editing spread into everyday tools.
- Playback and shared document histories became less exotic.
- The lesson became a classic product warning: demos can win applause while users still ask, "But where does this fit in my day?"

## Why It Is Interesting

Wave is a beautiful disaster because it was not empty hype. It contained pieces of the future. They just arrived later, chopped into smaller tools with clearer jobs.
